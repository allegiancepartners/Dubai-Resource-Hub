Appendix A — DIFC Wills Service Registry & Forced Heirship

The Structural Fact With No US Analogue · Illustrative Example · 2026

The Default Regime

Absent an election, matters of inheritance and succession in the UAE are governed by Sharia principles, applied through the UAE's personal-status framework. This produces fixed, rules-based shares for specified heirs — a materially different starting point from the testamentary freedom most Western UHNW clients assume, and one that can conflict with a family's actual wishes, particularly where a non-Muslim spouse, a blended family, or unequal distributions are involved.

The DIFC Wills Service Registry

The DIFC operates a Wills Service Registry allowing individuals (historically framed around non-Muslims, though the service has broadened over time) to register a will, under a common-law framework, that is recognized by UAE authorities as governing the disposition of their UAE assets and, in some cases, guardianship of minor children — in effect, an opt-out of the default Sharia distribution for the assets and matters it covers.

Why This Is the Organizing Axis for Segmentation

  • Resident UHNWI — UAE-domiciled assets and family are most directly exposed to the default regime; DIFC Will registration is typically the first, highest-priority action.
  • Non-Resident UHNWI — UAE-situated assets may still fall under UAE succession rules even though the family's primary domicile is elsewhere — a frequent source of unintended conflict-of-laws exposure.
  • Transnational UHNWI — may face forced-heirship or reserved-share rules in more than one jurisdiction simultaneously, requiring coordinated (not just UAE-specific) planning.
Illustrative note: the precise scope of DIFC Will registration (which asset classes, which family circumstances, which court will ultimately apply it) is a matter for DIFC/ADGM-qualified counsel to confirm for each family — this appendix is a starting orientation, not a substitute for that advice.

Appendix B — DIFC Foundations

Asset Holding, Succession & Governance · Illustrative Example · 2026

What Is a DIFC Foundation?

A DIFC Foundation is a separate legal entity (more civil-law "foundation" than common-law "trust" in character) that can hold assets in its own name, governed by a charter and by-laws, administered by a council, and used for succession planning, asset protection, and philanthropic or family-governance purposes.

Why It Fits UHNW Succession Planning

  • Assets transferred into the Foundation are held by the Foundation itself, outside the founder's personal estate — removing them from the default forced-heirship distribution that would otherwise apply.
  • Councils and guardians can be structured to reflect family governance wishes across generations.
  • Onshore DIFC domicile, common-law framework, and DIFC Courts oversight give it credibility with international counsel and counterparties.

Considerations

  • Requires ongoing governance (council meetings, record-keeping) — not a "set and forget" structure.
  • Best paired with a registered DIFC Will covering any assets that remain outside the Foundation.

Appendix C — ADGM Foundations & Trusts

Abu Dhabi's Parallel Regime · Illustrative Example · 2026

Overview

ADGM, Abu Dhabi's financial free zone, offers both a Foundations regime (broadly comparable to DIFC's) and a Trusts regime under its own common-law framework. For a family already anchored in Dubai/DIFC, ADGM is typically considered when a specific feature (a particular trust structure, a specific advisor relationship, or asset type) makes it the better fit — not as a default alternative to DIFC.

Appendix D — Golden Visa Pathways

Long-Term UAE Residency · Illustrative Example · 2026

What the Golden Visa Offers

The UAE's long-term (commonly 10-year, renewable) residency visa program extends eligibility to investors, entrepreneurs, and other qualifying individuals, without the need for a national sponsor and with the ability to sponsor family members. It has been a significant driver of the UHNW migration into the UAE described in the Dubai/UAE Wealth Management Roadmap.

Common Qualifying Routes (illustrative — confirm current criteria)

  • Real estate investment above a specified threshold
  • Public investment / deposit thresholds, or ownership of a UAE-based company meeting specified criteria
  • Specified professional, executive, or specialized-talent categories
Eligibility thresholds and categories are periodically updated — confirm current criteria with UAE immigration counsel before advising a specific client.

Appendix E — UAE Tax Overview

Personal vs. Corporate · Illustrative Example · 2026

The Headline Advantage

The UAE levies no personal income tax and no personal capital gains tax — a fundamentally different value proposition from the US practice's estate/gift/GST-tax-driven planning. The UAE's wealth-management pitch is built on succession certainty and structuring, not tax-exemption arbitrage.

ParameterValue (illustrative, confirm current)
Personal income tax0%
Personal capital gains tax0% (in the individual's personal capacity)
Federal corporate tax (introduced 2023)9% on taxable profit above the small-business relief threshold
Small-business relief thresholdA specified profit threshold below which the 0% rate continues to apply
Free-zone qualifying incomeMay continue to benefit from a 0% rate on qualifying activity — confirm scope with tax counsel
Because there is no estate or gift tax, the UAE structuring conversation is about certainty of distribution and cross-border coordination — not minimizing a tax bill the way the US Wealth Transfer Toolkit's Estate Freeze analysis does.

Appendix F — Cross-Border PPLI for UAE-Connected Families

Illustrative Example · 2026

Where It Fits

For Non-Resident and Transnational UHNWI who remain subject to income or estate tax in a home jurisdiction, Private Placement Life Insurance — the same structure detailed in Allegiance's US Reference Library, Appendix K — can wrap tax-inefficient holdings (hedge-fund-style strategies, frequent trading) inside a policy that grows income-tax-free in that home jurisdiction, while the UAE side of the family's structure handles succession certainty through a DIFC Will and/or Foundation.

  • Most relevant where the family has real, ongoing tax exposure elsewhere — not a UAE-only concern.
  • Allegiance's existing PPLI structuring relationship (Malcolm Dermit, International Wealth Protection, Zurich) is directly applicable here, since PPLI structuring is inherently cross-border.
  • Requires coordination between UAE counsel, home-jurisdiction tax counsel, and the PPLI structuring partner — a natural fit for the Transnational segment in particular.
